hey

so, it's done. btech is over. four years, compressed into a degree, a few hundred photos and a group chat that's slowly going quiet. and now i'm in a startup, waking up to standups instead of attendance sheets, and somewhere between the last exam and the first sprint, i blinked.

that's the only way i can describe it. a blink.

> Life moves pretty fast. If you don't stop and look around once in a while, you could miss it.
>
> — Ferris Bueller, _Ferris Bueller's Day Off_

i used to think Ferris was just a kid skipping school with style. turns out he was the most honest philosopher of the 80s.

## the great trade-off

when we're kids, all we want is to grow up. stay up late, decide our own dinner, have money, have freedom, have nobody telling us what to do. adulthood looks like the ultimate unlock, the final level where all the good stuff is.

and then you get there, and you realise the final level comes with its own boss fights. rent. deadlines. emails that start with "just following up". the freedom is real, but it ships bundled with responsibility, and there's no option to uninstall that part.

so now, the same kid who couldn't wait to grow up quietly wishes to be younger again. not for the age, but for the lightness. back when the biggest worry was homework, and summer felt like it lasted a year.

funny how that works. we spend half our life wanting to be older, and the other half wishing we were younger. almost never do we just want to be exactly where we are.

## the college patch notes

college was its own version of life. a sandbox, really.

late nights that weren't about work. conversations that started at 11pm and somehow ended at sunrise. trips planned in five minutes, assignments done in five minutes before the deadline. canteen debates about things none of us actually understood. that one friend who always said "last one, i promise" and never meant it.

none of it felt important at the time. that's the trick. the moments that end up mattering never announce themselves. they don't come with a loading screen saying "this one's a core memory, save it". they just happen, quietly, on a random tuesday, and only later do you realise that was the good part.

## will we meet again?

honestly, maybe. maybe not.

people scatter after college. different cities, different jobs, different time zones and different versions of themselves. we'll say "let's catch up soon" and some of us will mean it, and some of us will actually do it.

but i've stopped thinking that's sad. in the first post here, i wrote that every person we meet leaves a line of code in us. college was a massive merge of those commits. the way i argue, the way i laugh, the things i care about, the way i handle a bad day: so much of it was written by people i shared those four years with.

even if we don't meet again, those lines are already in the codebase. they run every day. that's not losing someone; that's carrying them.

## so, look around

now i'm at a startup, where everything moves even faster. weeks feel like days, months feel like weeks, and there's always something urgent pretending to be important.

which is exactly why i want to hold on to Ferris's advice a little tighter. not to slow life down, because you can't, but to actually notice it while it's happening. the chai between meetings. the walk back home. the dumb joke someone makes when everyone's tired. the version of me sitting here right now, writing this, who'll be a memory soon too.

so if you're reading this from somewhere in the middle of your own blink: stop for a second. look around. this, right now, is one of the moments.
