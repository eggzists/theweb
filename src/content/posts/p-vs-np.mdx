In this post I will be discussing P vs NP, a very interesting topic in computer science and mathematics. I mean why wouldn't it be? It's one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, a set of problems stated by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, and if you solve any of them you get a prize of $1 million.

Till date only one of those seven problems has been solved: the Poincaré Conjecture (which, again, I will be covering in another blog). So the P vs NP problem is still unsolved.

## so what is P vs NP?

Basically it asks whether every problem whose solution can be verified quickly can also be solved quickly.

"Quickly" here means it can be computed in polynomial time (`O(n)`, `O(n log n)`, etc.), and the class of problems that can be solved in this bounded time is known as class **P**. But some problems cannot be solved in polynomial time, yet if you're given an answer, it can be verified in polynomial time. We will use some examples to understand this problem with more clarity.
